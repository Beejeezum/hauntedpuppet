# The Ghost Who Tried to Be Tough

Story revision requested by the user. Supersedes the 49-second assembly as the narrative target. This is a script and production brief, not a finished film.

Wisp is genuinely sweet and playful. His scary form is the same being showing off, not a separate evil character. One brief serious scare is followed immediately by an embarrassed, cute recovery. Most of the performance remains visual; the two short character lines below are the explicit story-specific exception to the earlier nonverbal ambient brief.

## Transition and projection rules

- Locked camera and pure black stage. No generated horizon, lit room or rectangular full-screen smoke. All fog belongs to Wisp, the letters or the bats.
- Target 20% clearance around the full body, every wing and every emitted wisp. Do not crop to conceal clipping.
- The scary reveal is one brief story beat, not a repetitive long scare. Keep the operator’s immediate Stop/Blackout path and a future clearly labelled gentle variant.
- Morphs need their own coherent acting and matching exact-media joins. No doubled-body fade. Recover from the roar in the same action before beginning the apology.
- At departure, ten-percent size is the stopping scale, not a waypoint on the way to a tiny dot. Turn, counterbalance the hands, bank into depth, then phase out locally.
- Typography must spell HAPPY HALLOWEEN exactly. Designed letters can be composited through a reviewed vapor material/matte; do not ask a video model to improvise spelling. Letters must fragment and advect, not fade intact.
- Bats start behind the smoke and approach the viewer; their wakes visibly break the smoke. Dissolve/bank the bats before the projection boundary. End fully empty with no orphan wing, eye, spark, haze or sound.
- Lock each visual performance before spending on final voice and synchronized effects. Reuse usable motion and sound where appropriate; buy one focused missing proof at a time, not a batch of prompt variants.
