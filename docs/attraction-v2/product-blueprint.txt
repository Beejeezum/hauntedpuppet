YARD APPARITIONS — SHOW AND PRODUCT SCOPE
Revised 2026-10-29 after the human narrowed the project to projected floating ghosts
and spooky eyes in the yard. Proposed production plan within the confirmed boundary
in yard-apparitions-scope.txt. This replaces earlier product blueprints.

THE EXPERIENCE
A beautiful little haunting appears to live in the yard. Wisp is a friendly spirit
who takes his haunting duties a little too seriously. He watches, drifts, tries to
look frightening, occasionally succeeds spectacularly, then returns to his softer
self. His spooky form is the same being. Floating eyes suggest he is still there
even when his body is gone. The story is readable through acting, without dialogue.

A passerby watching for ten seconds gets a good visual moment. Someone staying for
two or three minutes sees a small arc. Neither must participate, hear a long script
or start at the beginning. The mango tree is the setting, not a game or character.
No lantern, sensing challenge, guest quest or lighting installation.

VISUAL LANGUAGE
Preserve approved painted soft-fog Wisp. Body overlap, hands, eyes and silhouette
changes carry acting; moving a static picture around does not qualify. Scary Wisp
holds a menacing presence before a stronger reveal. Use a few deliberate positions
and one main focal subject at a time. Optional secondary eyes peek elsewhere in the
same projection area. Their pupils/lids act; they do not bob as a rigid pair.
Do not fill the screen with smoke or duplicate Wisp into a busy collage.
Depth comes from authored turns, foreshortening and travel. Departure retains a
readable small body before vaporizing; no shrink-to-dot or abrupt resizing.

THREE SHORT SCENES
1. Somebody is there, roughly 20–40 seconds. Wisp materializes, inspects the yard,
   notices the viewing direction, leans or gives a small wave, then resumes living
   idle. An eyes-only watch is an alternative atmospheric scene.
2. Practicing his haunting, roughly 30–50 seconds. Friendly Wisp tries a little boo,
   is unimpressed with himself, gathers courage and transforms. A sinister held
   glance or slow reach supplies the payoff. He relaxes back into cute form with a
   sheepish look. A stronger short scare is an explicit operator option.
3. Still here, roughly 20–40 seconds. His body disperses but the eyes remain, glance
   sideways and blink. He reforms, or the eyes disappear before an intentional
   empty beat and another authored arrival. These alternatives need reviewed routes;
   do not cut the current movie at an arbitrary blink.

Scenes are reusable story units, not mandatory back-to-back loops. Ambient idle or
eyes can continue between them. Happy Halloween smoke lettering and a bat rush are
an optional later finale, not a dependency for the basic haunting.

SIGNATURE SHOW — ABOUT TWO AND A HALF MINUTES
This is the Mixed edition. Friendly uses playful beats without a scary morph;
Spooky can remain ominous and resolve to eyes/retreat instead of cute recovery.
Editorial timing targets, not locked generation lengths or permission to pad footage.
00–20: An authored friendly arrival and small look around establish life.
20–50: Quiet floating business and a wave; let the face and body breathe.
50–75: A failed boo, anticipation and change into sinister Wisp.
75–100: Scary presence, slow approach/reach, then body dispersing into floating eyes.
100–125: Look/blink, body reforming, then cute recovery and embarrassed acting.
125–145: A little goodbye and a properly turned departure into vapor.
145–150: Deliberate empty space before the next arrival.
Provide enough actual moving performance; do not tile a five-second idle to fill
thirty seconds. Shorten the show if good footage cannot sustain the proposed length.
Optional finale edition adds a separately authored lettering/bats ending and may
run closer to three minutes. It is not required for the first release.

Deliver the fixed signature show as ONE seekable local video with ONE embedded,
continuous audio track. It plays as one file inside and outside the app. Review its
repeat boundary. Keep separate source performances and sound stems for editing.
The interactive version uses admitted performances and joins; it is a separate
deliverable, not a playlist passed off as the finished master.

OPERATING MODES
AUTO HAUNT: Friendly, Spooky or Mixed; pacing, sound level and quiet intervals.
Friendly favors cute residency/play. Spooky favors sinister residency/eyes. Mixed
dwells in each form and uses an authored morph before changing. Initial editorial
targets: 20–45 seconds of residency between major actions, 90–180 second form dwell in
Mixed, no immediate story repeat. Tune by viewing, not by padding weak loops.
The strongest reveal is manual by default. No sensors or cloud calls are required.

DIRECT: a small phone performance surface: Show ghost, Eyes, Hello, Spook, Change
form, Vanish, Resume auto and persistent Blackout. A library drawer exposes the
individual available performances. Clearly show Playing, Queued and unavailable
routes. A tap replaces the uncommitted next action instead of stacking stale sends.
Normal requests wait for a reviewed handoff; Stop acts immediately. Reconnection
must not replay earlier commands. The phone remains silent.

PLAYLIST / FIXED SHOW: choose a saved ordered performance sequence or the single
signature video. Insert only reviewed connectors. Flag unavailable routes before
playback. Support repeat and intentional quiet holds. Never promise arbitrary
movies blend simply because they are both selectable.
Initially the fixed master is atomic: interrupt it by controlled fade or Blackout,
then use a deliberate restart/arrival. Seamless mid-film branching waits until
authored chapter exits exist. Do not jump to arbitrary timecodes.

SAY SOMETHING: retain short phone-typed speech as a separate second-stage feature.
Prepare voice and coherent mouth performance before starting. Bounded text, visible
preparing/ready/playing state and cancellation. Nonverbal auto playback keeps working
without speech services. Both voices and lip sync have their own acceptance gates.
Webcam and manual voice conversion remain archived.

PROJECTION AND SOUND
Laptop owns one clean secondary-screen projector window. Setup includes placement,
size, permitted travel area, optional mirror, basic corner mapping and masking,
layer opacity and saved calibration. Fit the actual existing projection surface;
ground effects and floating apparitions are distinct looks. No free-air promise.

Layer order: black base; optional localized fog behind; hero ghost; optional eyes
or foreground accent. Initially one hero, one eyes layer and one restrained fog
layer. Each allowed layer has placement, opacity and show/hide. Keep calibration
separate from live control. Prevent live dragging from breaking a validated route's
framing. No unlimited editor/plugin system or multi-projector edge blending.

Use true alpha where present. Test black-backed films in the chosen compositor so
they do not cover other layers with rectangles or wash out black. Black pixels do
not automatically mean transparency. Protect hands, glow and smoke with the 20 percent
reserve target. Remove grid, cursor and controls from the public output.

Sound stays sparse and dry: air movement, dissolve/reform, restrained growl/reveal,
occasional wings. No choir or compulsory musical bed. Preserve tails through joins;
intentional quiet is allowed, dropout or chopped sound is not. Nonverbal show is
default; a voiced story edition is separate. Laptop is the only audible owner.
Projector and phone never add duplicate audio.

TRANSITIONS — THE MAIN PRODUCTION WORK
Friendly idle -> friendly action -> friendly idle.
Friendly idle -> authored morph -> scary idle.
Scary idle -> scary action or body/eyes performance -> scary idle.
Scary idle -> authored recovery -> friendly idle.
Explicit departure -> intentional empty/eyes interval -> authored arrival.

Compatibility includes full pose, movement velocity, framing, lighting and audio
tails. Matching names or one similar frame is insufficient. An autonomous eyes
overlay is separate; it is not permission to splice the body/eyes movie.
Author compatible motion, preload, preserve picture/sound clock, then inspect joins
at normal speed. Dissolves can soften fog or empty space; doubled faces cannot hide
a mismatched character cut. No compulsory fade after each ordinary action.

CONTENT BUDGET
Keep the existing 16 performance families as a maximum first library, not a quota
for 16 new paid generations. Existing resident-contract IDs remain unchanged:
friendly-drift, friendly-wave, friendly-boo-fail, friendly-depart, friendly-reform;
scary-drift, scary-pace, scary-reach, scary-eyes, scary-retreat, scary-fakeout,
scary-reform; cute-to-evil, evil-to-cute; fog-lettering, bats-through-letters.
Prioritize both resident looks, both morphs, wave, friendly arrival/departure and
the eyes effect. Distinct prowl/fakeout and final lettering/bats follow that proof.
Do not relabel duplicates to fill slots. Bridges and loop repairs count toward
cost; 16 family names do not mean 16 files or 16 generations will suffice.

Audit/reuse standalone eyes and layer-ready fog first. The current scary-eyes movie
includes whole-body dissolve/reform; it is not already an independent looping eyes
layer. A compatible eyes-only hold and clean compositing must be proved.
Each paid revision targets a named defect, uses the authorized workspace, records
actual debit and is reviewed before further spend. No batch generation this pass.

IMPLEMENTATION PARTS
1. Local package: source, playback versions, hashes, frame/duration data, bounds,
   alpha/black mode, sound stems/mix and exact join reviews.
2. Scene compositor: calibration, bounded layers and independent placement.
3. Show director: resident states, compatible routes, profiles, variation and Stop.
4. Phone bridge: private pairing, acknowledgment, anti-replay and disconnect recovery.
5. Separate speech service once its coherent character proof passes.
Reuse tested modules where useful. Choose one playback/compositing owner. Adopt a
native renderer only if exact-media testing proves visible or reliability benefit;
do not require QLab+OBS+TouchDesigner together. Cached local shows need no cloud
session or generation spend. Secrets stay in the trusted service.

DELIVERY ORDER AND EXIT CHECKS
A. Stage proof: existing Wisp and separately placed eyes; calibration, boundary,
   compositing, opacity, persistent output and immediate Blackout. Label candidates
   honestly. Inspect rectangles, identity, contrast and full fog bounds.
B. Resident mini-show: friendly action, both morphs and compatible eyes path, with
   actual loop/join sound review. No frozen settle or unplanned black. A missing
   arrival bridge is a named gap, not a generic fade disguised as completion.
C. Auto/manual operation: phone pairing, safe takeover/return, profiles and saved
   sequence, visible queue, sound ownership and connection-loss recovery.
D. Short story: coherent picture/audio master exported as one file; inspect the
   whole story and three repeats. Use approved routes rather than hardening the
   timeline around whatever clips happen to exist.
E. Expand admitted content up to 16 families; optional lettering/bats. Speaking
   remains a separately tested follow-on, not a reason to withhold a useful visual
   show or claim the full speaking-character objective is complete.
F. Night rehearsal: actual projector/material/viewing angle, spill, readability,
   sound and real phone. Two-hour 1× unattended run, ten takeover/return cycles,
   network loss, minimized laptop, closed/reopened output. Separate simulated,
   decoded-media, human art/audio and actual installation evidence.
