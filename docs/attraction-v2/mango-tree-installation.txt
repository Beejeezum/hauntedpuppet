LAYOUT TO PROVE
1. Mark the guest approach and the strongest viewing area. Keep the screen plane
   and any supports outside the walking route. Measure usable width/height.
2. Temporarily position mesh under the canopy, with dark space behind it. Compare
   front and rear projection on the actual material. Rear placement is an option
   only if space, focus, transmitted-beam glare and guest sightlines permit it.
3. Check light passing through the mesh: a second ghost on the tree/house ruins the
   effect. Change angle, placement or a concealed beam catch rather than assuming
   black pixels extinguish the projector's physical light output.
4. Put the sound near Wisp's apparent position. Keep any tree accent light off the
   mesh and avoid light/fog that exposes the screen or projection beam. Full canopy
   illumination during every cue is unnecessary. A brief motivated glow can help.
5. Use a stable support/tension arrangement so breeze does not wobble the character.
   The tree canopy is not weather protection for the projector: keep it dry with
   the ventilation required by its manual. Final outdoor setup needs on-site checks.

  inspect the canopy, look toward approaching visitors, sway with a motivated beat.
- Play: react to a small falling spark/imaginary mango, investigate it, then make a
  sheepish glance toward guests. This is a variant brief, not an extra paid cue yet.
- Scary: darkness builds within the same form; a forward lean/reach is readable
  from the sidewalk. Do not enlarge beyond the measured safe effect envelope.
- Travel: turn the head, shoulders and whole body into the path before moving in
  depth; stop at a readable small scale and vaporize. No shrink-to-dot departure.
- Finale: letters hang in the tree's open dark space, disperse locally into vapor,
  small quick bats come from behind them. All light is contained by the usable
  scrim, including smoke trails and glow; no bright rectangle or scenery backdrop.

  cannot work, stage disappearances within the open space rather than fake an
  occlusion that breaks when a guest shifts position.
