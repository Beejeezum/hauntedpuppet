RECOMMENDED PREMISE: THE TREE STOLE HALLOWEEN
Wisp is the keeper of an old lantern beneath the mango tree. Mischievous spirit-light
has escaped into the tree. Visitors help gather it. Wisp steals a little too much
courage, discovers his scary side, and needs their help to settle down again.

The physical installation consists of one bounded ghost/effects projection plane,
one practical glowing lantern, a few controllable tree lights, nearby sound, and an
interaction zone on the existing guest path. Guests need no phone or instructions
longer than one short invitation. No assumption of free-air volumetric projection.

THE PLAY LOOP, ABOUT 45–75 SECONDS PER GROUP
1. Discovery. A quiet tree, breathing lantern and occasional ghostly habit. An arrival
   produces a restrained response. Wisp offers one invitation: "Help me catch that."
2. Connection. A slow hand motion bends a cloud of spirit-light on the projection
   plane. Light begins responding immediately, before any reward animation. This
   cause-and-effect connection is the first crucial proof.
3. Cooperation. One guest can guide the cloud into the lantern-aligned target;
   two guests can gather it faster. Slower, steady movement holds the cloud together.
   Fast movement scatters it playfully, with an easy return and no punishment.
   The physical lantern brightens continuously as light is gathered, and a branch
   responds. No numeric arcade score or ever-growing HUD on the apparition.
4. Temptation. Gentle mode produces a silly brave pose. Mischievous/Haunted can
   offer a visible invitation to try a "monster pose" or dare Wisp. A sustained,
   deliberate response unlocks the scary branch; random movement cannot trigger it.
   The operator can always hold the friendly route or deliberately add a stronger
   reveal. Never infer a guest's age or tolerance from camera appearance.
5. Recovery. Bringing hands down slows the live field, warms the lantern and brings
   Wisp back. If nobody responds, he recovers himself after a short fixed maximum.
   Optional short embarrassed line. Guests caused a different ending than another
   group, rather than merely advancing the same playlist.
6. Release. A small reward of mothlike spirit lights, warm tree glow and a goodbye.
   Wisp returns to his own habits. People can join halfway, watch only, or walk away;
   no one is trapped in a long dialogue or a failed minigame.

WHAT THE CONTINUOUS MECHANIC ACTUALLY IS
The guest's current hand/zone position drives a force field in real time. Simulated
particles have position, velocity, lifetime and attraction to a moving target.
Cloud cohesion, lantern charge and gust strength evolve continuously with input.
SFX intensity/position and the physical light level follow those same state values.
Story events happen at explicit thresholds and completed animation boundaries.
It must still respond if every character reward movie is temporarily disabled.
Merely detecting a wave and then playing a prerecorded effect fails this brief.

THE PHONE BECOMES A SECRET MAGIC CONTROL
Two modes: conductor and unattended.
Conductor has a touch area to guide the spirit field, a bounded intensity control,
an invitation/hold/release choice, a guest note or exact short line, and Stop.
Example: notice a guest dressed as a witch, type "The witch has stolen my sparks,"
and guide the live field toward that part of the stage while Wisp responds.
No promise of automatic costume identification or arbitrary speech-ready film.
The operator may assist an interaction invisibly if sensing is uncertain. Such a
run is honestly operator-assisted, not reported as autonomous tracking success.

Unattended uses observed presence and coarse movement after night testing. It
invites one interaction at a time, has bounded challenge/recovery times, remembers
recent encounters only as anonymous session state, and varies its own quiet habits
when nobody participates. With no reliable sensor it runs an ambient show, not a
fictional conversation with imaginary guests. Network loss leaves local effects,
story logic and cached fallback operational. Phone and projector remain silent.

OTHER DISTINCT MECHANICS TO TRY AFTER THE FIRST PROOF
- Scare School: visitors teach an insecure ghost a monster pose; he overachieves;
  they calm him again. Coarse pose/energy, anticipation and comic timing matter.
- Ghost Keep-away: moving left/right changes where escaped light travels; Wisp
  tries to steal it, then gives it back. No running or chasing into the yard needed.
- Haunted Lantern: a large physical touch/control point gathers light. This is
  the simpler sensing fallback if camera tracking is unreliable in the installation.
These are variations of a tested reactive world, not a commitment to three extra
products, dozens of paid films or three more operator interfaces.

TECHNOLOGY DIRECTION — CANDIDATES, NOT AN INSTALLED STACK
TouchDesigner is the lead native prototype candidate for the reactive environment,
compositing, mapped output and coordinated lighting. Its GPU Point Operators can
simulate/modify live geometry; the official example networks provide a practical
starting point. Use Mac-compatible POPs and shaders. Avoid Nvidia Flex/Flow,
Nvidia-only Optical Flow TOP, Windows-only tracking plugins and hardware ray tracing
on this Mac. Benchmark the actual network before naming it the production engine.
Non-Commercial is restricted to personal/noncommercial work and1280x1280 images;
a full1920px-wide final output requires an appropriate other license. Nothing bought.

AI'S USEFUL ROLE
Short contextual character lines, a guest note from the operator, and bounded
intent interpretation. It does not drive the particle loop, decide emergency stop,
or invent arbitrary lighting/renderer commands. Use selected character voices and
prepare their speaking performance before playback. Ambient play costs no AI credits.

THE CHARACTER LIMIT WE MUST NOT HIDE
Our beautiful films cannot become arbitrary live body/face/eye animation by loading
them into TouchDesigner. Reuse the best footage for deliberate acted reactions and
payoffs. Live field movement and physical lighting must work independently. Full
free-form Wisp acting requires a separately authored coherent rig/material/face
system and has its own art gate. Do not resume pasted-on mouth/hand patches, warp
the whole image to pretend he turned, or let particle spectacle mask a weak puppet.
Speech can initially be a short theatrical line during an appropriate authored
performance, but that is not accepted live lip sync for both characters.

Runway Characters/LiveKit is a documented whole-video conversation option, but
our prior two bounded Wisp auditions failed speech articulation (fixed grin or
closed mouth) and cost4credits total. The current docs do not overturn that local
evidence. Do not buy another audition automatically or advertise it as our working
puppet. Vendor24fps and1.75second server-side response claims are not our benchmark;
the documented5minute session cap and ongoing billing also matter for a yard show.

GATES FOR THE NEXT PROOF
- Guests understand the cause/effect in five seconds without reading a tutorial.
- Provisional target: input-to-visible response p95 below150ms, measured on the
  actual target setup. This is a design target, not a benchmark already achieved.
- A motion reversal changes the field before a movie boundary; a still hand has
  a visibly different effect from frantic motion. Inspect actual recordings.
- Run the same initial state with different inputs: outcomes must differ; disabling
  reward movies must not remove the core interaction.
- Preserve the coherent body and complete20%effect border on the scrim. Branch
  lights provide spatial extension; projection does not leave its real surface.
- No input/noise/confidence drop cannot produce repeated scary reveals. Stop blacks
  projection, ends sound and returns practical light to a defined calm state.
- Frame/render, physical input, audio, and human-play evidence are reported separately.
