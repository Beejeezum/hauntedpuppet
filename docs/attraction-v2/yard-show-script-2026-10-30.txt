WISP — “JUST PRACTICING”
Production script v1 · 2026-10-30 · planned, not a finished show file

THE EXPERIENCE
A ghost has claimed a little patch of the front yard. He is trying very hard to be
frightening. His disguise works a little too well, embarrasses him, and he goes back
to welcoming visitors. A spider above him gets the last scare. Guests do not have
to stop, play a game, hear an explanation or see the whole sequence to enjoy it.

Under the mango tree: darkness is the stage, not a background picture of a tree.
Everything luminous belongs to the apparition, eyes, silk or lettering. Show no
ground plane, rendered landscape, full-screen fog, illuminated rectangle or UI.

TARGET LENGTH: 2:40 /160seconds, followed immediately by the next cycle.
This is an edit plan. Final duration follows the approved acting, never arbitrary
speed changes. One continuous picture/audio master is the signature-show deliverable;
the same chapters also have clean state contracts for interactive cue operation.
Do not call a browser playlist with independent audio files that final master.

00:00–00:12  WATCHFUL DARK
One pair of eyes opens off-center. It glances toward passing guests, hears a tiny
rustle elsewhere and looks down. A second pair appears lower and farther away;
the first blinks shut. Never travel the eye layer while it is visible. Leave enough
negative space for guests to wonder whether they saw anything.
Sound: one close dry rustle, tiny air movement. No musical pad or choir.
Dialogue: none. End: eyes go dark; an intentional one-second empty beat.

00:12–00:32  OH, HELLO
Friendly Wisp emerges from his own fog near center, then notices the path. The
body keeps moving while the head looks, the eyes settle, and one hand waves.
Proposed line, quietly delighted: “Oh! Hello. I’m practicing my haunting.”
Hold enough friendly idle afterward for the line to land. Do not jump to another
pose on the last word or make a mouth patch chase the head.
Sound: soft breath-like arrival, dry hand-swish only where the arm actually moves.
End: friendly idle, same scale/location, native body/fog motion still running.

00:32–00:55  THE REHEARSAL
Wisp drifts a little sideways, listens, tries to look dignified and loses his
nerve. He gathers himself, looks left/right for witnesses, then toward guests.
Proposed line: “All right. Watch this.”
Sound: sparse cloth-like air and one tiny inhale. No constant effects wallpaper.
End: friendly morph-entry pose; velocity and silhouette match the transformation.

00:55–01:08  TOO GOOD AT IT
The same Wisp turns genuinely sinister through the approved painted morph.
He holds the threat for a beat, looms just a little, then gives ONE short creature
roar. No enormous volume jump, strobing, sudden crop or screen-filling face.
The maximum threat lasts roughly2seconds, then he realizes he overdid it.
Sound: a short dry rising breath during change, one synchronized roar with a
rounded transient, quick release. No sustained demonic dialogue or music sting.
End: scary return-morph entry. Do not cut directly to a cute film.

01:08–01:29  SORRY ABOUT THAT
The reverse morph reveals sheepish Wisp. Hands drop a little; eyes glance toward
the visitors before an embarrassed smile. Keep the character physically continuous.
Approved performance reference: the earlier apology video, not today's rejected
Fable/Onyx test voices.
Line: “Oops! Hope I didn’t scare you. I’m just trying to be tough.”
Sound: the growl drains into a small exhale; leave room for the actor's laugh.
End: friendly idle; optional short wave or listening beat after speech.

01:29–01:50  SOMETHING ABOVE
A tiny silk creak draws Wisp's eyes upward. He withdraws into his own fog.
After he has completely cleared, a spider descends from the overhead anchor,
balances on the thread, reaches with two forelegs, then actively climbs away.
Do not play the currently rejected spider studies as final footage. Both generated video studies remain rejected. The new articulated painted
spider preview supplies descent/reach/climb now; final human art approval is pending.
Sound: faint dry silk tension and small leg scritches; no comedy boing or large
impact. End: silk and spider vanish into darkness before crossing a picture edge.

01:50–02:10  MY LITTLE SECRET
Friendly Wisp cautiously reappears, checks that the spider is gone, then gives a
small pleased flourish. Body remains translucent and legible, lower core intact.
Proposed line, conspiratorial: “You didn’t see that.”
Optional variation, not in the same performance: “He’s the scary one.”
Sound: tiny breath and one airy flourish. End: native friendly settling pose.

02:10–02:31  SEE YOU AROUND
Lettering forms from narrow moving threads of vapor: HAPPY HALLOWEEN. It must
retain porous edges and moving interior smoke. Wisp looks toward it, then guests.
Line: “Happy Halloween!”
He turns away, travels into depth while still recognizable, then loses cohesion
into fog. Stop recession while roughly1/10 original size, never a vanishing dot.
Neither lettering nor ghost may resize abruptly. Typography disperses as smoke
advects apart; an opacity fade alone does not meet the requested material.
Current runtime lettering is a functional text proof, not the approved smoke film.

02:31–02:40  LEAVE THEM WONDERING
Small quick bats rush outward from behind the remaining letters, break up the
smoke and pass. Use the reviewed smaller framing; never clip the source smoke.
Dry wings pass with motion, a restrained squeak and a fading air tail. Finish
fully black with a short quiet gap. The next cycle begins with eyes elsewhere.
No hard musical ending or repeated loud “finale” for the neighbors every160seconds.

THREE RUN MODES — SAME OPERATOR SURFACE
Friendly: holds the cute resident ghost. Replace the scare/morph passage with
friendly curiosity and a wave. Omit sinister eyes and spider unless explicitly
enabled separately. No unexpected evil reveal while the user chose Friendly.
Evil: stays scary. Longer watches, restrained roaming, reaching/looming, sinister
eyes and dark pauses. Minimal voice; no accidental apology/cute source leakage.
Mixed: this story, including the deliberate transformations and recovery. Start
the morph only at its compatible authored entry, queue requests during a morph.

AFTER-DARK MODES ARE SEPARATE SMALL SHOWS
Watching eyes: staggered native performances, new positions in darkness, varied
spacing/dwell. Already implemented as a local silent rehearsal.
Quiet apparitions: a resident ghost with slow travel, a planned disappearance,
two dark beats, return. Already implemented using existing native Wisp footage.
Thread lurker: new painted eight-legged spider with jointed descent/reach/climb
and a dark dwell is now a local preview. Human appearance approval remains open.

HOST MOMENTS (SHORT, OPTIONAL, EXACT TEXT)
“Excellent costume. Mine’s permanent.”
“Come closer. I promise I’m mostly friendly.”
“I was terrifying a minute ago. You missed it.”
“Tell the skeletons I’m on my break.”
“Happy Halloween. Watch out for my upstairs neighbor.”
Use one line, then let the performance breathe. No AI monologues, generic greetings
on every loop, unsolicited improvisation or mandatory microphones/cameras.

VOICE DIRECTION / NEXT CASTING PASS
Friendly: a small warm original spirit, impulsive curiosity, audible thought and
comic hesitation; clear vowels without squeaky chipmunk pitch. Different intentions
within one short line, not one cheery narrator tone. The apology is the benchmark.
Evil: the same intelligence turns dry, close and dangerous. A creature texture
supported by clear consonants; do not cover poor acting with heavy echo or bass.
The brief menace must be able to fall away into the friendly character immediately.
First approve one short A/B audition per form; then test the same voice in welcome,
apology and threat. Do not buy a full script before identity and consistency pass.

ASSET AND HANDOFF CONTRACT
Each asset specifies: character form, neutral/acting entry and exit, world-space
placement, full subject/fog bounds, native frame rate, source hash, audio ownership,
approved material, and maximum allowed hold. A movie ending in darkness is an
empty-stage cue, not a universal bridge between ordinary actions.
Seamless resident joins require compatible drawings AND motion at both boundaries.
Crossfade cannot repair different face shape, doubled limbs or mismatched velocity.
The show master embeds one mixed audio stream. Voice sits above dry effects; tails
cross chapter boundaries in the actual mix. No browser-timer soundtrack stitching.
For a phone line: synthesize first, hold appropriate resident form, then play once
from the laptop. Cancel/blackout stops it everywhere. Projector and phone are silent.
